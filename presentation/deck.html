Key design assumptions
  • – Maya is the first line of defense and reviews every analysis.
  • – Derek can open any analysis, but his job is resolving escalations.
  • – The system recommends; humans keep decision authority.
  • – Information is disclosed progressively, by responsibility.
  • – Every decision and escalation reason stays traceable.
!Working assumption

Near limit → escalate. If the value is within spec but its uncertainty range crosses a limit, Maya sends it to Derek.

e.g. #201 porosity 35.6%, range 35.2–36.1% vs. spec 32–36%. Still to validate with technicians and quality.

What I Simplified or Hid

Q: What did I deliberately simplify, omit or hide, and why?

PART 1
Hidden from Maya
Uncertainty ranges, repeat scans, lab-reference charts.Why: she needs a decision, not evidence to interpret. Derek sees all of it.
Any confidence %, for anyone.Why: Maya can't act on it, and Derek doesn't trust it.
Override of a fail suggestion needs a reason.Why: Accept against rework is the costly mistake — keep it intentional and traceable.
Simplified
Uncertainty becomes three outcomes: pass, fail, second look.One rule: range inside, across or outside spec. Quality can tune it.
"Range" = min–max of eight passes, not a ± interval.Why: Derek sees the raw spread; a CI would imply false precision.
Escalation reason: five choices + optional note.Why: fast for Maya, sortable for Derek.
Left out
Batch release, trends across batches, shift handover, notifications, accounts.Why: outside this results screen.
Real uncertainty data.Ranges and lab coupons are illustrative; production models output point values today. The brief asks to design as if the signal existed.
Rule of thumb: show each person only what changes their next action.

Uncertainty 1: Repeatability

Physical question: if we scan the same coupon again, does the reading move?

PART 3
#203 · Mass loading · repeat passes
| = one plasma pass, fixed setup  ·  reported value
7 of 8 passes above 15.5; 1 in spec. Spread 15.46–15.70 (0.24 mg/cm²).
mg/cm² · illustrative repeat-pass data · min–max, not a ± interval

What's physically happening

Plasma stability and sensor noise shift each pass slightly under a fixed setup.

What Derek can conclude

Most passes read high, but one lands in spec — noise alone could explain a 0.1 excess.

What it can't tell him

Repeatable isn't accurate. A calibration bias would repeat on every pass.

Next step if the spread were wide

Check the setup and re-scan. More passes narrow random noise.
Shown as the raw spread of passes against the limit, not as ± or a percentage.

What's physically happening

The model learned from coupons weighed in the lab. Few have loadings this high, and the high-end pairs scatter more.

What Derek can conclude

Up here support is thin, so a consistent bias is possible — not proven.

Why it differs from repeatability

Routing uses the repeat-scan range only. Reference coverage is evidence for Derek; it is naturally thin near any limit.

Next step
